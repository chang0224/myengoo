# Remote Work, Not AI, May Be Driving Unemployment

#### ¶1

The rise of remote work has made businesses more reluctant to hire young, inexperienced workers and is the key driver of higher unemployment rates for recent college graduates in the US, a new study has found.

#### ¶2

The study, by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, compared occupations that can be done remotely — such as software development — with those that are done in person, such as nursing. The study found that the unemployment rate among young college graduates in "remotable" jobs rose by about 1 percentage point from 2017-2019 to 2022-2024.

#### ¶3

Yet for older workers in those fields — aged 29 and over — the unemployment rate declined slightly. As a result, there is a notably higher unemployment rate for younger workers in remotable occupations compared with older workers.

#### ¶4

But in non-remotable jobs, there isn't a gap in the unemployment rates between older and younger workers.

#### ¶5

The study concludes that businesses are reluctant to hire new college graduates into remote work because it is harder to train them outside of the office. The study found that remote work is responsible for nearly two-thirds of the rise in the unemployment rate for young college graduates since the coronavirus pandemic.

#### ¶6

The New York Fed study also looked at detailed data from an unnamed tech company and found similarly consistent hiring patterns.

#### ¶7

When the company's offices were closed and staff worked remotely, "the firm hired fewer inexperienced workers and more experienced workers, who might need less mentorship to do their jobs well," the study said.

#### ¶8

"Once its offices reopened, the company shifted back to hiring younger workers," the study said. But even after the reopening, the company favored more experienced workers for teams that included remote work.

#### ¶9

Currently, there is widespread concern over the increasing automation by AI of entry-level white-collar jobs in areas such as finance, law and media.

#### ¶10

But the study notes that the employment situation for young college graduates was getting worse before the development of AI tools such as ChatGPT. And the study found that AI had little impact on unemployment among young college graduates.
