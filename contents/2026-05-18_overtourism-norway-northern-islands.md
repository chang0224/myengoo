# Overtourism Hits Norway's Northern Islands

#### ¶1

In recent years, residents living in popular holiday destinations have complained about overtourism.

#### ¶2

And it's not just people in warm-weather places like Barcelona, the Greek island of Santorini or the beaches of Thailand. Because as the world gets warmer, tourists have also been looking for "coolcations" — vacations to places where the weather is cooler.

#### ¶3

For example, just a few years ago, not many tourists had heard of the archipelago of Lofoten in the north of Norway.

#### ¶4

Called "the world's most beautiful islands," Lofoten is a place where the mountains meet the sea, and the sun is out all night during the summer!

#### ¶5

And these beautiful islands are becoming better known.

#### ¶6

According to the Nordic research website Nordregio, 1 million people visited during 2023, the most recent year for which data is available. That's a lot of visitors — especially when these islands have fewer than 25,000 actual residents!

#### ¶7

And slowly, the people of Lofoten have begun to complain about their increasing number of visitors.

#### ¶8

Einar Benjaminsen is the mayor of Flakstad, a municipality near the western end of the archipelago. In 2025, he told Sveriges Radio: "Ten years ago, maybe 2% of locals didn't want tourists — today it's more like 25%."

#### ¶9

Lofoten resident Frida Berg told the BBC that she doesn't like the litter and traffic that can come with tourism. "This is our home — not just a backdrop for a Facebook selfie," she said.

#### ¶10

But tourism is also really good for the economy — and residents know this too.

#### ¶11

Erling Hansen, a Lofoten tour guide, told the BBC that tourism is keeping the archipelago's villages alive.

#### ¶12

However, if visitor numbers increase even more, could those same tourists smother these beautiful islands?
