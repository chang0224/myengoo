# Nuclear Power Is Making a Comeback — Here's Why

#### ¶1

Forty years ago, the Chernobyl disaster cast a long shadow over nuclear power, slowing its development across the world. Chernobyl's Reactor Number 4 exploded on April 26, 1986, contaminating nearby areas and spewing radiation across Europe.

#### ¶2

Today, however, there's a growing nuclear revival — a global trend given a big boost by the energy crisis caused by war in the Middle East.

#### ¶3

Over 400 nuclear reactors in 31 countries now provide about 10% of global electricity, with about 70 more reactors under construction.

#### ¶4

Leading the revival are the United States and China. The US, the world's largest nuclear power producer with 94 operational reactors, plans to quadruple its capacity by 2050. Close behind, China operates 61 reactors and leads the world in new builds, with nearly 40 under construction.

#### ¶5

Though there is disagreement within Europe on nuclear power, the European Commission now sees nuclear power as a clean energy essential for climate goals. In 1990, a third of Europe's electricity came from nuclear, now it's only about 15%. But European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen recently said it was "a strategic mistake" for Europe to turn its back on nuclear power.

#### ¶6

The EU is considering the development of Small Modular Reactors, which would be cheaper and faster to build.

#### ¶7

While Germany and Italy have outlawed the technology, France is the EU's largest nuclear power producer, generating 70% of its electricity from 57 reactors, and has plans to build six more.

#### ¶8

Russia, meanwhile, has 34 operational reactors, is constructing new units at Kursk, Leningrad and Smolensk, and is also building 20 reactors in Europe, Africa, Asia and the Middle East.

#### ¶9

Ukraine still relies on nuclear plants for about half its electricity, despite the risks of active war. And Japan has restarted 15 reactors while reviewing lessons from the Fukushima nuclear accident.

#### ¶10

Fatih Birol, executive director of the International Energy Agency, told The Associated Press "I am 100% sure nuclear is coming back."

#### ¶11

"It's seen as a secure electricity generation system, and we will see that the comeback of nuclear will be very strong," he said.
